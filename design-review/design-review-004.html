Design Review 004

Verdict Screen Narrative Flow. Round 3 (full-depth 5-panel review), STRICT criteria.

Verdict
Build with caveats

The 5-beat direction is correct and independently confirmed as the right fix across all 5 panels, but the proposal-as-sketched has real gaps that must be resolved before implementation, not deferred. Two critical gaps (beat 4's forced grouping and the binding-constraint deletion premise) were independently identified by separate panels and are load-bearing to the restructure. Build sequence supplied; implementation requires explicit resolution of these two points and specification ambiguities at 4 sites before code starts.

Executive Summary

Following explore-006 and design-review-003 (which fixed jargon leaks and fold mechanics), the user reported the verdict screen "still doesn't flow properly, it should have a story." A 5-beat restructure was proposed following the Minto Pyramid Principle (decision → next action → basis → what-could-change-it → the record) to replace the current 19-block field-ordered layout. This design review independently stress-tested that proposal via 5 parallel panels (A, C, D, E, G; no Panel B/interface-contracts or Panel F/quality-attributes as noted below). Every panel independently confirmed the core diagnosis is real—decision restated 4–6 times before the reader finds the thread; two competing in-page navigation devices—not exaggerated. But two separate panels independently converged on the same defect in the proposed fix: beat 4 is a forced grouping with real conceptual misfit, and folding it by default hides exactly the "could this break" information most-likely readers want visible. A second independent convergence: the premise for deleting the binding-constraint block is factually wrong, which would move required disclosure from always-visible to hidden-behind-a-fold for specific audiences.

Panel D confirmed one real, unconditional test break (the in-page nav bar's existence is asserted by a test) requiring deliberate test update, not silent deletion. Panel C flagged latent technical debt (Fold's defaultOpen is mount-time-only) that the restructure doesn't fix. Panel G flagged a scope gap: a tier/track chip-before-legend finding from design-review-003 sits outside this restructure's stated scope and risks being left as unaddressed debt.

Scope & Methodology Note

Panel B (Interface Contracts) did not run: this is a pure UI reorganization with no change to the actual component API or contract between VerdictDisplay and RegisterDetail, and no new fields enter or leave that interface. Panel F (Quality Attributes / ATAM Utility Tree) did not run: no new quality-attribute scenario is introduced; the existing utility tree from the original build covers performance/scalability/availability already and nothing in this restructure changes those tactics. Both gaps are explicitly scoped out, not oversights.

Critical Findings

Two critical issues, both proposal-design gaps (not existing-code bugs):

1. Beat 4 grouping is not a real conceptual unity and should not be folded by default.
Panels C and G independently converged on this defect. Panel C: "fragility, inheritance, and living-status are being placed there for beat-count symmetry" — only expiry-conditions genuinely matches "what could change this"; fragility is present-tense (today's margin, not a future trigger), inheritance is provenance of already-granted controls, living-status is a current-state readout closer to "the record." Panel G, independently: folding this beat by default hides "this decision is not permanent and could break" from exactly the audience most likely to want it — recommends promoting beat 4 to always-visible (changing the visible-by-default set from 2 beats to 3), and renaming it to name its actual content directly ("What could go wrong / when this expires") rather than the scan-ambiguous "What could change this."
VerdictDisplay.tsx §beat 4 (proposal location)
Fix: Split beat 4 into its true parts — expiry-conditions gets its own always-open beat; fragility joins beat 3 (basis — it's about the strength of the reasoning); inheritance and living-status join beat 5 (the record — provenance and status readout). Rename beat 4 to "When this expires" and render it always-open, not folded.
2. The binding-constraint block deletion is based on a false premise and risks a real disclosure regression.
Panels A and D independently converged on this defect. Panel A: VD-2 (Must) requires the exact binding invariant be shown; if the standalone block at VerdictDisplay.tsx:910–916 is deleted and its only remaining rendering falls inside the beat-3 fold (closed by default for non-2LoD per reasoningDefaultOpen), a 1LoD submitter loses default visibility of information VD-2 requires — RA-9's audience-dependent-fold exception explicitly does not extend to VD-2. Panel D, independently: the premise itself is wrong — VerdictDisplay.tsx's appetite line (lines 860–873) never renders the binding_constraint code or binding_path at all; it states only control/review counts. There is nothing to be "redundant with."
VerdictDisplay.tsx:860–873, :910–916 (proposal location)
Fix: Do not delete the block; either keep it in beat 1 (compact, one line) or explicitly carry binding_constraint + binding_path into whatever beat-1 summary line replaces it, and confirm it renders regardless of role/fold state. Verify that VD-2's disclosure requirement is satisfied by the final DOM.

Important Findings

Six important issues requiring attention:

3. Section-nav deletion breaks a real, confirmed test and removes a deliberate anti-rubber-stamp design decision.
Panel D confirmed the test break at VerdictDisplay.r15c2.test.tsx:67–74 — nav's existence and its lack of a sign-off link are directly asserted. Panel E, independently: the nav's code comment records a deliberate decision that "a one-click path from page-load straight to Approve is the rubber-stamp risk the design panel rejected" — if the nav goes, the sign-off checklist becomes the sole place enforcing "no direct jump to sign-off," and that discipline should be stated explicitly there, not assumed.
VerdictDisplay.tsx:~line (nav section); test at VerdictDisplay.r15c2.test.tsx:67–74
Fix: Update (not silently delete) the r15c2 test in the same commit as the nav removal, and add a one-line comment at SignOffChecklist's definition recording that it is now the sole no-direct-jump-to-sign-off enforcement point.
4. "Extend reasoningDefaultOpen to more Folds" and "wrap beat 3 in a plain section" are underspecified.
Panel D. VerdictDisplay.tsx has 7 Fold call sites; only 1 currently takes the reasoningDefaultOpen prop. The proposal doesn't name which of the other 6 "more" refers to — a real behavioral fork. Separately, Fold's when={false} fallback path renders a bare fragment and drops the id prop entirely (confirmed at VerdictDisplay.tsx:100–135) — a developer using when={false} to get a "plain section" instead of a wrapping Fold would silently lose the anchor id the checklist/nav jump-links depend on, a silent bug.
VerdictDisplay.tsx:100–135, multiple Fold sites (~7)
Fix: Name every Fold call site by its current title string that should adopt reasoningDefaultOpen; specify the beat-3 wrapper as literal JSX (a real <section> with its own heading, not a Fold with when={false}), preserving each inner panel's own Fold/id.
5. The proposal's own count of "restated 4 times" undersells the defect, but its fix is confirmed correct.
Panel G traced 5–6 distinct textual restatements of the decision (provisional banner, sign-off checklist's first bullet, section nav, h2 heading, appetite line, WhatToDo's lead sentence) before new information appears. Beat 1's proposed consolidation directly resolves this and is confirmed to extend — not regress — design-review-003's WhyThisVerdict fold fix (VerdictDisplay.tsx:918–935). This is recorded as Important only because the undercount itself should be corrected in any build ticket describing this work, so scope isn't underestimated.
VerdictDisplay.tsx multiple locations
Fix: Correct the count to 5–6 in any build ticket; build beat 1 exactly as scoped, no further consolidation needed beyond what's proposed.
6. A design-review-003 finding (tier/track chip-before-legend ordering) sits outside this restructure's scope.
Panel G. The proposal only reorganizes inside VerdictDisplay.tsx; RegisterDetail.tsx's chip/legend ordering (logged as an Important finding two rounds ago) would remain unaddressed while the team believes "the story is fixed" screen-wide. This is not a defect in the current proposal, but a scope-gap risk.
RegisterDetail.tsx:562–602 (outside proposed scope)
Fix: Either fold this into the current restructure's scope, or explicitly carry it forward in calibration.md as still-open so it isn't silently dropped.
7. Several "above/below" deictic copy references will break silently once panels move.
Panel D — enumerated via grep + manual read, distinguishing real cross-panel references from false-positive numeric/threshold uses of the words. Five real instances requiring explicit handling: VerdictDisplay.tsx:198 (Why-panel primer, forward reference to regulatory chain), :334 (WhatToDo rejected-path, forward reference to Why/regulatory chain), :1088 (jurisdiction "no chain" block, backward reference to Why-panel citations), :1161–1163 (controls "as-of" line, backward reference to the verdict heading), :1322–1326 (provenance panel, backward reference to the expiry-conditions Fold — the most fragile, since beat 4's internal reordering directly threatens it).
VerdictDisplay.tsx:198, :334, :1088, :1161–1163, :1322–1326
Fix: Resolve each individually as part of the beat-4/beat-5 regroup, not as a generic "fix the copy" pass. Keep a checklist of all five locations in the build ticket.
8. showSubmitterAffordances gates two separate UI elements with one boolean.
Panel D. VerdictDisplay.tsx:697 comment references FN-004 as the reason a "Correct this classification?" button and the reasoning-trace details were deliberately kept under one gate. If beat 5 relocates the reasoning-trace details away from the correction button, the restructure must keep both conditioned on the same onCorrect !== undefined check, not accidentally fork it.
VerdictDisplay.tsx:697
Fix: Verify after the beat restructure that both affordances remain gated together and document why in a code comment.

  3. Checklist reposition (no test currently pins its position): Move to beat 1 if the proposal calls for it.

Cross-Panel Themes

Theme 1: Independent convergence on beat 4's construction defect (finding 1) — Panels C and G, scanning for entirely different defect classes (structural soundness and audience hospitality), independently arrived at identical concrete observation: beat 4 is a forced grouping that doesn't fit its assigned conceptual role. This is the strongest evidence in this review that this specific part of the proposal needs to change, not just be caveated.

Must be resolved in the build ticket, not left as "TBD in code".
