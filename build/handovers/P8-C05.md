## Code Review Findings

**Review passes:** `[(1, 0), (2, 1), (3, 2), (4, 0)]`

Every finding across all four passes was the same defect wearing different
clothes: **the panel claiming more absence than was true.**

**Pass 1 — 0 findings.** It did answer the user-facing question this time
(added to the prompt deliberately after P8-C04), and noted a "read it twice"
risk: the banner and the body panel said nearly the same sentence. Acted on
without waiting for a later pass — the banner label was shortened to a terse
labelled cause, leaving the prose to the body.

**Pass 2 — 1 Important.** The panel said *"the tier, the controls and the
constraint above all come from that [the firm's policy]"*. False on a hard-line
rejection: `evaluate()` skips tier/track assignment and reports Critical/I as
**ceiling values** — verified at `src/engine/evaluate.ts:69-92`, whose own
comment says "not assignments". Reworded.

**Pass 3 — 2 Important, and the sharper catch.** The replacement wording
— *"nothing above comes from a regulator's rules"* — was **also false**, and
more broadly: the firm's own policy carries 31 `regulatory_basis` citations
(verified by grep over `policy/appetite.yaml`), which render directly above the
panel. Pass 3 also found the reason the new test could not catch it: the
fixture set `binding_regulatory_basis: null`, the one field whose emptiness made
the claim look true. Fixed both — the sentence now scopes to the jurisdiction
pack and affirms the firm's own citations, and the fixture carries a real
citation.

**Pass 4 — 0 findings**, plus a documentation note that became FN-003.

**And one more, found by eye rather than by review.** After pass 4 converged, I
read the rendered page against its own citation list and saw the banner still
said *"so no regulatory rules were used"* — the same overclaim as the body
panel's earlier drafts, just terser and in a place three review passes had
stopped looking. Now *"so no country rulebook was used"*, with a test asserting
the banner does not make the wider claim.

## Retrospective
Four review passes, and the finding that mattered most came from none of them:
it came from looking at the screen. Three independent passes read the same
banner string and cleared it, because each was checking the sentence against
the spec rather than against the page it sits on — and the page had a real
regulator citation printed a few centimetres above a sentence saying no
regulator rules were used.

The near-miss underneath it is worth more than the fix. Pass 3's second finding
was that my test could not have caught my own error, because the fixture left
`binding_regulatory_basis` null — the single field whose emptiness made a false
claim look true. That is the same shape as this project's oldest defect class:
not a wrong assertion, but an assertion that cannot fail. Worth a standing
habit — when a test proves a negative, check whether the fixture is what makes
the negative true.
