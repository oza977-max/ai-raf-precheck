## 0. Principles the build must keep

1. **Situation, not categories.** No question, option, summary line or first-screen sentence shows engine vocabulary: data class, data zone or zone letters, autonomy level, bindingness, model-type names (LLM, ML, deep learning, generative AI, agentic), tier, track, MNPI, PII, 2LoD, invariant, control ids, rule ids. The engine's words stay in the reviewer section, where they already carry plain glosses (NF-11).
2. **The engine does not change what it decides.** The only engine changes are PE-9 (a node attribute may hold a list) and stable ids on engine-generated reviews (§1.3). Rules change only through the policy file.
3. **Stricter when unsure.** Every "Not sure" maps to the stricter reading and is listed back to the user as an assumption.
4. **Honesty.** Nothing on the first screen claims more than the verdict supports: provisional stays provisional, attested stays "not verified", "in place" means machine-verified only.
5. **Reserved words.** No rendered string may contain "approved" or "rejected" (a UI test asserts a single match on the verdict screen). Never "immutable" in audit copy; "different histories", never "fork".
6. **Boundaries** (specs/cross-cutting.md §7): the answer→graph mapping is pure logic in `src/engine/`; components only present.

## 4. Chunk D — the verdict's first screen (VD-9, VD-10)

A new presentation component at the top of `VerdictDisplay`. Everything that renders today moves, unchanged, into a fold titled **"For your AI risk team — the full reasoning behind this result, rule by rule, with sources"** — collapsed for the submitter (1LoD), open for 2LoD (existing `reasoningDefaultOpen` behaviour). Interactive parts (owner assignment, attestation, sign-off) keep working inside it.

Content, in order (text generated from the verdict + policy plain fields + graph; the reference generator is `gen_v21_verdict.py` in the v2 test kit):
1. **Headline**
   - status out of appetite (hard line or unsatisfiable): **"No — not as described."**
   - needs sign-off (stage pre_checked) with N outstanding safeguards: **"Not yet. You can start once your AI risk team has signed it off and all N safeguards are in place."** (N=1: "…and 1 safeguard is in place."; N=0: "…has signed it off.")
   - self-service with N>0: **"Nearly. You can start once N safeguard(s) is/are in place — no sign-off needed."**; N=0: **"Yes — you can start."**
   - N counts **outstanding** safeguards: neither machine-verified nor attested — the same split the sign-off checklist calls "addressed" (verified + attested) versus outstanding. Attested safeguards are not hidden and not called verified: they appear after the list as "Reported in place by {name} (name not verified): …", so the reader and the signing reviewer see exactly what rests on a person's word.
2. **Why:** the distinct plain reasons of the tripped rules, binding constraint first, at most two, joined with "; and", then "(Each safeguard below gives its own reason.)" when there are more.
3. **Your next steps** (numbered):
   - sign-off needed: "Send this result to your AI risk team — the independent team that checks how the firm uses AI. They review the use in principle and sign it off; the safeguards can be finished after."
   - safeguards: all yours → "Put the safeguard(s) below in place — it's/they're yours to arrange (you or your manager)."; some yours → "Ask the team named against each safeguard below to put it in place, and agree a date. K of them is/are yours to arrange — marked \"yours\" and listed first. \"You or your manager\" means your side of the business: agree between you who takes each one."; none yours → "…None of them is yours to do yourself."
   - other teams' checks exist → "Also send this result to {teams} — they run their own checks, listed below. Ask each team whether you must wait for theirs before you start."
   - `no_regulatory_basis` → "Tell us which countries it involves, then check again — the answer may change."
   - sign-off + safeguards → "Start only when both are done: it's signed off, and every safeguard below is in place."; sign-off only → "Start once it's signed off."; self-service → "Then you can start. It's saved on your firm's register of AI uses, which your AI risk team can see."
   - when sign-off needed and safeguards exist: "Not sure who these teams are? Ask your AI risk team when you send them this result."
4. **Safeguards that must be in place before you start (N)** — outstanding controls, yours first: plain_action; "Who: …" (+ **yours** tag); "Because …" (the plain reasons of this control's tripped invariants — one line, or a short list); and, when `covers_reviews` matches a review on this verdict, "(This also covers {review plain name} — one piece of work.)". Then "Already in place: … (your firm's records show this)" for machine-verified controls, and "Reported in place by {name} (name not verified): …" for attested ones.
5. **Checks other teams run** — downstream reviews not covered by an outstanding or in-place control; plain name + team; de-duplicated by source id.
