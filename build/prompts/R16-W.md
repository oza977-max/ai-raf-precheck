## 1. Flow — the guided-form path

**W-1 Q2 is pre-filled (D-67).** The submitter types a description on the
first screen, then the form's question 2 asks for it again, blank.
`StructuredForm` takes `initialDescription`; question 2 starts with it
(editable) unless the form's own draft or `initialAnswers` (W-4) already
holds a question-2 value. The text the person leaves in question 2 is the
description carried forward from then on (reducer state, the register
node's description, the summary) — `GRAPH_EXTRACTED`'s form-path
replacement (W-3) carries it.

**W-2 One introduction (D-68).** The form shows two stacked intros: the
old "Guided intake — answer the fields below…" paragraph (with its Settings
parenthetical) above the approved R16 intro. Delete the old paragraph.
Keep the approved intro exactly; add one sentence after it as its own
paragraph: *No AI reads your answers or makes the decision, so the same
answers always get the same result.*

**W-3 The form path skips the old field-card screen (D-69).** After the
form's Continue, the app shows `graph_review` — the field cards (DATA_ZONE,
MODEL_TYPE…) with "not found in your text — worth a second look" on every
value. On the form path that screen is engine vocabulary the redesign
removed, and the "not found in your text" flag is false (the person picked
every value). The form path goes form → (questions, if any) → (contradiction
review, if any) → confirmation summary:
- New reducer action `FORM_SUBMITTED { graph, useCaseId, description,
  plainAnswers, assumptions, questions, contradictions }`, valid only from
  `graph_extraction` with `method: 'form'`. The caller computes, from the
  graph in hand (never from stale state): the reference check
  (`checkPolicyReferences(policy, loadedPacks)`; on errors, stay on the form
  and show the existing gate message — do not dispatch), `questions =
  generateQuestions(graph, policy, [])`, and `contradictions =
  detectContradictions(description, [], graph)`. Reducer: questions → the
  `questionnaire` state; else contradictions → `contradiction_review`
  (built the way `CONTRADICTIONS_DETECTED` builds it); else → `confirmation`.
  Pure; no audit write in the reducer.
- `GRAPH_EXTRACTED` stays for the description path only.
- `STEP_BACK` from `questionnaire` on a form-path graph
  (`graph.intake_method === 'structured_form'`) returns to the form
  (`graph_extraction`, method `form`, with `plainAnswers` — W-4), not to
  `graph_review`.
- **Similar decided cases** (R8-SC) are computed today only on
  `graph_review`. Also compute them on `confirmation` when the graph came
  from the form, and render the same collapsed panel there (after the
  summary, before the optional note; same `SimilarCases` component, same
  posture line). The description path is unchanged.
- `EVALUATION_FAILED` and `CORRECT_VERDICT` still land on `graph_review`
  for every graph — out of scope here (chunk E makes that screen plain;
  chunk D2 owns correcting a form-path verdict, see §5).

**W-4 Changing an answer reopens the form filled in (D-70).** Today
"Change an answer" (and Back) reopen a blank form, and a refresh on the
confirmation step loses the "Not sure" list (`formAssumptions` is a
`useState`, never persisted).
- Carry `plainAnswers` (the `PlainAnswers` object) and `assumptions` in the
  reducer state: optional on `graph_extraction` (form), `questionnaire`,
  `contradiction_review` and `confirmation`; set by `FORM_SUBMITTED`;
  preserved through every transition between those steps; `CHANGE_ANSWER`
  and the form-path `STEP_BACK` return them to `graph_extraction`. The
  intake draft already persists the reducer state, so a refresh keeps both.
  Delete the `formAssumptions` `useState`; read `state.assumptions`.
- `StructuredForm` takes `initialAnswers`. Precedence on mount: the form's
  own saved draft (in-progress edits) → `initialAnswers` → blank (+ W-1's
  `initialDescription` for question 2).
- **One use case, one creation event.** Every form submit today mints a new
  `useCaseId` and appends a new `use_case_created` — so "Change an answer"
  then Continue leaves an orphaned creation event on the append-only trail.
  On a resubmission (the state already holds a `useCaseId` from this
  intake), reuse that id and do not write `use_case_created` again. Test:
  submit, Change an answer, submit again → exactly one `use_case_created`
  for the intake, and the confirmed use case has that id.

**W-9 Which zone a firm platform runs in (D-79) — the backtest case
UC-6b parity fix.** Q3's platform option maps the zone to "the earliest letter among
its allowed zones" (R16 §2.2). For `PLAT-INTERNAL-ML` (allowed Zone B and
Zone C) that is always Zone B, so UC-6b — a deal-memo tool that runs
entirely on the in-house platform — gets HL-002's "No" where the worked
case expects it to pass. Add a follow-up, shown only when the chosen
platform allows more than one zone:
- id `3platformZone`; question: *Does your information stay on your firm's
  own systems the whole time?*; help: *Your firm's platform can run some AI
  itself and pass other work to an outside supplier. If you don't know
  which happens here, choose "Not sure".*
- options, only for zones the platform allows, in this order: Zone C *Yes
  — the platform runs the AI on the firm's own systems* → Zone C · Zone B
  *No — the platform passes it to an outside supplier's AI* → Zone B ·
  Zone A *No — it goes out to a public website or service* → Zone A · *Not
  sure* → the earliest letter among the allowed zones (assumed: *"it may
  pass your information to an outside supplier — the stricter case."*;
  when the earliest allowed zone is A, say *"an outside website or service
  — the strictest case."*).
- Required when shown; cleared when Q3 changes. A platform allowing one
  zone keeps today's mapping with no follow-up.
- Parity: the answer for UC-6b was decided outside this chunk, from the
  narrative only ("deal content never leaves firm-controlled
  infrastructure", `backtest/use-cases.md` UC-6b): the Zone C option
  ("Yes — the platform runs the AI on the firm's own systems"). Add it to
  `backtest/worked-case-answers.json` as exact option text (the file's
  convention) with a reason line in the same style as the others; no other
  worked case picks a multi-zone platform, so no other answer changes.
  UC-6b's listed difference should disappear — if it does not, report why
  rather than adjusting expectations. Add mapping and assumption tests in
  `plain-intake.test.ts`.

## 2. "Here's what we understood" — the form's own words (D-71)

The summary reads the graph through the reviewer cards' labels, so it says
"Personal details of clients" for "Information about people…", "Background
only" for "carries little weight", "Day-to-day internal processes" for
"None of these — it's for day-to-day work", "via unregistered (personal
account, no firm contract)" for the supplier, and never shows the answer to
"can a mistake be put right?" or the kind of AI. Add a `SUMMARY_VALUES`
section to `plain-copy.ts` (graph value → sentence, derived from the
newcomer-tested question wording) and render the summary from it. Still
graph-based (one truth: what gets evaluated), both paths. Exact text:

**Where your information will go** (heading unchanged)
- Zone A: *An outside website or service, outside your firm's control.*
- Zone B: *A supplier's systems, outside your firm's own.*
- Zone C: *Your firm's own systems.*
- Then, when the processing node's vendor is set and is not `internal`:
  *Through: {name}.* — `{name}` = the registry entry's `plain_name` when the
  vendor id is a policy platform/vendor id (neutral "a supplier on your
  firm's list" if it has none); otherwise the vendor string as recorded.
  For an unregistered vendor add a second line: *Your firm hasn't assessed
  this supplier yet.*
- When the processing node names a policy platform: *Runs on: {platform
  plain_name}.* (verify the field name on the processing node type).

**The information it will use** — every kind, most sensitive first:
- Client PII: *Information about people — clients, applicants, staff or
  anyone else who can be identified*
- MNPI: *Price-sensitive information — anything that could move a share
  price*
- Confidential: *Confidential firm information*
- Internal: *Everyday work information, or only what you type in yourself*
- Public: *Only public information*

**What it does and who sees it** — in this order:
1. Kind of AI (`model_type`): statistical *A score or forecast from fixed,
   written-down rules, like a scorecard* · traditional-ml *A score, ranking
   or forecast whose builders can show what drove each result* · ml *A
   score, ranking or forecast whose builders can't easily show why it gave
   a result* · deep-learning *AI that recognises things in images, sound or
   documents* · llm *AI that reads, summarises, translates, writes or
   answers questions in words* · generative-ai *AI that creates images,
   audio, video or code* · agentic *An AI agent that works through tasks on
   its own, using other tools or systems*
2. What happens with its output. If `autonomy_level >= 2`: level 2 *It
   decides or acts by itself, and a person reviews afterwards* · level 3
   *It acts by itself within limits someone set, with no routine review* ·
   level 4 *It acts entirely by itself, with no person involved at any
   point* — then, by `action_type`, append trade *— it places or changes
   trades* · approve *— it makes yes-or-no decisions, like accepting an
   application* · execute *— it sends, books, updates records or deploys
   changes* (no clause for other action types). If `autonomy_level <= 1`,
   by `action_type`: read *It finds or summarises things for people to read
   — nobody acts on it directly* · inform *It answers people's questions
   directly, like a chat assistant* · draft *It creates a draft* · recommend
   *It suggests, ranks or flags things* · execute/trade/approve *It prepares
   an action* — and when `hitl === true` append: draft *, and a person
   checks it before it's used* · recommend *, and a person decides what to
   do* · execute/trade/approve *, and a person approves each one before it
   goes ahead*.
3. Weight — only for inform/draft/recommend at `autonomy_level <= 1`:
   non-binding *What it produces carries little weight — nobody makes an
   important decision from it* · advisory *What it produces is one input
   among several when someone decides* · material *What it produces is
   usually what a decision is based on* · binding *What it produces is
   acted on without a person deciding*
4. Audience (`exposure`): internal-only *Only you or your own team see what
   it produces* · internal-shared *Other teams in the firm see what it
   produces* · client-facing *Clients or customers see what it produces* ·
   market-facing *The public, the market or regulators see what it
   produces*

**If it gets something wrong** (new section; `output_reversibility`):
reversible *The mistake can be caught and put right before it does lasting
harm* · irreversible *The mistake can't be taken back once it happens* ·
unknown *Not known whether a mistake can be put right*

**What it helps decide**: credit-decision and lending-decision *Whether to
lend to someone, or on what terms* · hiring *Who to hire or promote* ·
pricing *What to charge a client, or how something is priced or valued* ·
trading *Buying or selling investments* · fraud-detection *Spotting fraud
or financial crime* · regulatory-reporting *Figures or statements sent to a
regulator* · operational *Nothing specific — it's for day-to-day work* ·
`decision_type_other` *{text} — not one of the kinds we have rules for, so
your AI risk team will look at it* · none *Nothing in particular*

**How widely it's used, and where**: limited *Just you, or a small trial* ·
at_scale *Your team as part of normal work, or wider* · countries by plain
name; none *None of the listed countries — somewhere else, or you weren't
sure* · the "replaces" sentence unchanged.

**Agents** (unchanged sections, new wording): reach — none *Nothing beyond
what it's given for the task* · credentialed_systems *Its own logins,
passwords or access tokens for other systems* · deployment_authority *It
can change software or settings, or deploy updates, without a person* ·
shared_infrastructure *It runs on computers or servers shared with other
automated tools*; working together — check the field's actual values and
map: works alone *It works alone* · yes *Copies of it, or other AI agents,
pass work or messages to each other* · unknown *Not known whether copies of
it, or other AI agents, pass work to each other*.

Unchanged: the assumptions list, "Change an answer", and the collapsed
"Show the details the rules use" grid (reviewer vocabulary stays there).

**Supplier strings at the source (D-72).** `plain-intake.ts` records

**W-7 "Already in place" only where the evidence covers this tool (D-77).**

platform attestation on file") is firm-level, so every verdict that trips

- View-model: `verified` only when the evidence applies to this graph. When
