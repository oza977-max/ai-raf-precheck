Application Brief — AI Risk Appetite Pre-Check Engine

Date: May 2026

Prepared by: Kshitij Oza

Status: Draft — for review

1. Problem Statement

Banks are deploying AI faster than their risk frameworks can absorb it. Every business line wants generative AI. Every competitor is adopting it. But the Second Line of Defence (2LoD) — the risk function that governs AI adoption — is drowning.

The specific challenge is threefold:

Bottleneck.

Risk managers review AI tools the same way they reviewed spreadsheet models: Word documents, committee meetings, bespoke memos. A single use case can take three months to clear.

Inconsistency.

Because each review is a human judgment call, two identical use cases submitted by different teams can receive different outcomes. There is no canonical, repeatable answer to "is this in appetite?"

Blind spots.

Self-attested intake is gameable. A developer who says "internal productivity tool, no client data" when the tool actually summarises client calls and feeds into regulated records will pass most manual reviews.

2. Proposed Solution

A pre-check engine that sits between "I have an idea for an AI tool" and "we build it."

The use case is modelled as a data-flow graph. The bank's RAF is expressed as invariants over that graph. The engine returns: in/out of appetite, what tripped, the binding constraint, and the minimal control set — or reject.

The engine issues an attested certificate per use case — a living object with a status that can self-revoke if KRI conditions are breached after approval.

3. Value Proposition

For the CRO: AI risk review stops being the bottleneck. Every use case has an auditable verdict derived from the bank's own rules. For the 2LoD team: the engine handles mechanical classification; the team focuses on edge cases and policy. For the business: clear, fast answers on day one.

Key differentiator: not a one-time snapshot. The certificate stays green only while the controls hold.

4. Existing Landscape

Manual frameworks (Word / Big 4): expensive, slow, non-repeatable, stale. Horizontal AI governance tools (Credo AI, Holistic AI, ModelOp, Arthur): general-purpose, do not speak RAF/3LoD/MRM natively, not jurisdiction-aware at the SS1/23 vs SR 26-2 level.

5. High-Level Solution Shape

  • Type: Web application (MVP: client-side local; V2: hosted SaaS / bank-controlled deployment)
  • Users: CRO/Head of AI Risk (2LoD), AI developers (1LoD), Internal Audit (3LoD)
  • Scale: Team → department → enterprise-wide
  • Connects to: bank's RAF (config file), AI use case register, regulatory content packs (SR 26-2, SS1/23, EU AI Act, OSFI E-23, MAS FEAT), and (V2) live KRI feeds

6. Stakeholders and Users

Role Relationship to product
CRO / Board Risk Committee Approval authority; audience for register and certificate evidence
Head of AI Risk (2LoD) Owner and primary operator
AI developers / 1LoD Submit use cases; consume verdicts
Internal Audit (3LoD) Consume certificate evidence
Regulators Consume attested register on request

7. Constraints

  • Regulator quality or not at all — traceable, attested, immutable, reproducible
  • Bank's RAF is sacrosanct — engine reads the bank's own rules
  • No sensitive data leaves the tool (MVP: local)
  • Jurisdiction-aware by design
  • MVP: client-side, no backend, policy as a file

8. Possible Outcomes

Approval authority may: Approve / Approve with conditions / Absorb into existing project / Defer / Decline.
